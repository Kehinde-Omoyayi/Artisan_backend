# Drop 36 — private service recordings and production acceptance runbook

21 October 2026 · Product manual v1.0 · **Code delivery only. No live deployment, migration, bucket change or provider activation was performed.**

## Enforced product policy

- A customer/artisan SERVICE conversation has a legitimate customer and a real request FK from its DRAFT stage. A draft is not submitted demand, a booking, completion or payment. `submit_request=true` is the explicit Send Request action; ordinary messages/automatic parsing never submit it.
- New category chats and customer/artisan direct starts use that existing workflow. An eligible artisan can join an OPEN request through `POST /api/v1/conversations/start/` with `request_id`. The existing one-request/one-service-room architecture is retained; a different assigned artisan cannot silently take over an existing room.
- Both roles may send text, still photos, supported video or voice notes. Voice recording uses browser-negotiated MIME, stop/preview/discard/re-record and explicit Send, with upload/text fallback. Audio: 90 seconds and 5 MiB; video uploads: 60 seconds and 10 MiB. Backend signature, decoding, stream type, actual duration and size checks are authoritative. FFmpeg/FFprobe are in the backend Docker image. No AI, transcription, paid messaging provider or model training is involved.
- Quotes carry labour, materials amount/specification, transport, additional amount/breakdown, total, scope, schedule and derived duration, payment terms, exclusions and workmanship terms. A revision is a new parent-linked numbered quote, not an in-place change to accepted terms. Accepted financial terms remain immutable; bookings/payments retain independent authoritative statuses. An artisan's wording does not create a platform warranty, identity guarantee, escrow guarantee or automatic refund promise.
- **Booked deletion:** request COMPLETED **and** at least one legitimately owned booking COMPLETED, with no active booking and no active dispute/payment/chargeback/legal hold. Cancelled/rejected siblings alone never establish completion. Attended, archived, request submission and payment are not renamed completion.
- **Unbooked deletion:** the customer explicitly closes a request that has no booking. Audio/video becomes inaccessible and eligible for purge **seven elapsed days after `closed_at`**, unless a hold applies. No idle auto-closure or abandonment inference. Existing ARCHIVED unbooked history can be explicitly closed from its room; its archival date is not reused as a closure date.
- Still photos, ordinary text, transactions, quote/revision records, reviews and support history are not targets of recording retention. Recording-only messages become generic no-content system placeholders. New recording uploads stop after closure; text/photos/history remain available.

## What the code checks and deletes

Playback requires authentication, legitimate room membership, CLEAN scan state and a current policy check. API and session-authenticated frontend proxies return private/no-store responses; native audio/video elements support single byte ranges without signed public object URLs or bearer tokens in URLs. The frontend proxy buffers at most the already bounded uploaded file; it never redirects the browser to storage.

The worker locks the request, checks current holds, writes an independent minimal tombstone, removes recording-only namespace orphans, primary files, tracked derivatives and all exact-key versions/delete markers. AWS S3 replication destinations and explicitly configured historical replica buckets are included. Replica-only namespace orphans are enumerated, not forgotten after a failed primary deletion. Pending/failed replication, access denial, object lock, unknown backends or unverified invalidation keep references and RETRY_REQUIRED, never COMPLETE. A supported S3-compatible backend must expose the management/verification APIs used by these checks; an unverified alternative fails closed.

After verified deletion/invalidation, attachment and derivative references and temporary migration journals are removed. The purge audit retains only request reference, policy version, state, timestamps, attempts and audio/video counts. Failed cleanup retains the minimum actual retry keys in protected rows until it can finish; error alerts do not contain media names, keys, URLs, contents or decoder output. Generic deletion/cascades cannot bypass the request recording retention worker; administrative history and quote deletion are disabled.

Current and retired replication inventory must be confirmed by the operator. The application cannot discover destinations removed from historical configuration, third-party exports, recipient downloads or unmanaged backups. A provider's failed replication must be reconciled before a verified purge can finish.

## Exact rollout order — operator actions, not performed here

1. Back up source and transactional business data. Keep deployed `.env`, accounts, actual databases and uploads; the delivery contains no replacement production database or credentials. Keep `CONVERSATION_RECORDINGS_ENABLED=false`. Do not launch a second beat scheduler.
2. Inspect the real bucket policy, public-access blocks, object ACLs, versions, replication and retired destinations, CDN configuration, scanner, worker queues and backup rules. The repository expects S3-compatible production storage and the existing Celery/Redis/SMTP architecture; repository inspection does not prove the actual hosting configuration. Preserve SMTP SSL/465 and the cream/brown email layout.
3. On the existing database, run the read-only pre-migration report: `python manage.py audit_legacy_room_links`. Review ownerless/mismatched SERVICE rows and ambiguous old dual-role DIRECT rooms. Backfill stops rather than inventing an owner. For a real business room, record its legitimate existing customer/artisan; for a truly unrelated peer/staff room with ambiguous dual-role accounts, explicitly record `legacy_peer_or_staff_confirmed: true` in its existing draft JSON. Do not use that marker to exempt a business room. Preserve messages, participants and original request ownership.
4. Run `python manage.py migrate` under the normal API settings in controlled maintenance. New migrations are services 0008–0009 and conversations 0007–0011. Earlier cumulative migrations remain included. UUIDs for existing request-policy identities are backfilled individually, not with one shared callable default. Historical quotes receive ordered version labels without fabricating missing historical terms or completed jobs. Shared catalogue tables remain; this is not a cross-app catalogue-removal migration.
5. Run `python manage.py migrate_legacy_conversation_media` for a report, then `python manage.py migrate_legacy_conversation_media --apply --limit 500` repeatedly. The temporary journal commits both keys, digest and size before binary side effects. The private copy is checksum-verified before public-origin removal. Old versions/replicas and configured CDN caches must be verifiably revoked. Failure leaves a retry journal; do not delete it by hand. Photos are copied intact, not discarded by this privacy migration.
6. Provision `CONVERSATION_PURGE_LEDGER_PATH` on **private durable storage shared by all API and media-worker instances**, independently preserved from database/media snapshots. A container-local ephemeral file or different per-service disks is not sufficient. Run `python manage.py initialize_media_policy` once in the controlled rollout. It preserves prior tombstones and creates a private external identity receipt; it never overwrites a mismatched identity. Keep both the JSONL ledger and its `.identity` receipt durable. Do not reset/truncate them to get past a failed check.
7. Configure and verify provider chargeback notifications. Flutterwave documents `chargeback.initiated` and notes this notification service is available on request; confirm it for the actual account rather than assuming ordinary payment webhooks include it. [1](https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/chargebacks) The existing authenticated webhook creates a preservation hold from matching transaction/reference data. An unmatched authenticated chargeback stays pending and conservatively pauses all recording purges and payouts until reconciliation; payment state is not guessed from a chargeback notification. Unknown chargeback lifecycle notifications never automatically release evidence. Staff must verify the provider outcome, record a release reason and formally release the hold; that action records the staff actor and a minimal audit entry. Releasing a media hold does not itself resolve financial disputes, change payment status or reverse a past payout.
8. If no CDN is used, leave its flags false after confirming that fact. If one is used, implement/configure `CONVERSATION_MEDIA_CDN_PURGE_HOOK(request_id, attachments)` returning exactly `True` only after verified cache invalidation, and `CONVERSATION_LEGACY_CDN_REVOKE_HOOK(attachment_id, source_key)` for legacy revocation. A false/absent/unverified hook cannot complete a purge/migration. These are explicit adapter contracts, not a claim that any unknown CDN was already integrated.

Explicitly confirm `CONVERSATION_MEDIA_BACKUP_POLICY_CONFIRMED`, `FLW_CHARGEBACK_WEBHOOKS_CONFIRMED` and `CONVERSATION_MEDIA_REPLICA_INVENTORY_CONFIRMED` only after the corresponding evidence exists. Set `CONVERSATION_MEDIA_HISTORICAL_REPLICA_BUCKETS` for retired destinations. Then run `python manage.py conversation_media_preflight`. Production upload checks independently reject missing confirmations, legacy migration backlog, invalid ledger/identity and unverified private buckets. Physical retention purges also remain conservatively paused until the provider/replica/backup confirmations exist; feature-off is not used as evidence that the external configuration is safe.

Exercise staging end-to-end: both roles, permission denial, wrong MIME/signatures, 90-second/5-MiB audio boundary, supported mobile MIME, private byte-range playback, explicit Send Request, immutable accepted quotes/revisions, unread/general notification separation, provider-held payment state, both-state completion, seven-day explicit closure, each hold, partial storage failure/retry, replica-only orphan removal, CDN invalidation and a restore drill. Test PostgreSQL locking/concurrency and actual storage IAM. Synthetic SQLite/mocked-store tests here do not prove these host/provider properties.
11. Use the existing worker queues `celery,verification,maintenance,notifications,media,payments` and **one** beat process. Retention reconciliation is scheduled every minute; after-commit events enqueue immediate cleanup and hold-release rechecks. `python manage.py purge_service_recordings --limit 500` is the manual reconciliation path. Monitor oldest eligible/pending/retry ages, attempts and alerts against the **15-minute target**; failures retain blocked access and retry references, not a false success. Test the actual workload/capacity before asserting this SLO.

The optional Procfile/render blueprint contains no live secrets and keeps recordings off. ## Restore procedure — do not serve restored recordings first

Put services in maintenance; stop ordinary producers/workers. Restore the independently preserved **latest** deletion ledger and identity receipt before restoring/serving a database or media snapshot. Retain the same request-policy UUIDs. A missing/corrupt ledger or mismatched database policy identity blocks playback and purge rather than silently initializing a new history. A backup predating policy-identity migration needs a reviewed reconciliation, not a freshly overwritten receipt.

Run `python manage.py purge_service_recordings --restore-scan --limit 500` repeatedly, inspecting status/backlog. The restore scan includes tombstoned requests even when their restored request/booking states predate completion; it does not invent new completed states. Current active holds conservatively prevent physical re-deletion and require review, while the independent tombstone continues to deny playback. Verify primary/replica versions, retired destinations and caches again. Verify identity/sequence/UUID behavior for new requests: an old integer ID reused after restore must not resurrect or misidentify a newer request. Resume traffic only after reviewed acceptance.

Never claim immediate erasure from immutable backup vaults, snapshots outside application control, recipient downloads or third-party copies. Recording payloads must be excluded from inappropriate general backup/snapshot workflows or governed by an explicitly verified retention/restore process. Permanent deletion cannot be reversed by a database rollback. The code's default-off and fail-closed gates are deliberate, not a declaration that these operator duties have been completed.
